As AI agents take on more digital work, the risk isn't just fewer jobs. It's people getting shut out of an economy that's increasingly built to hire agents, not humans.
Say to Match is one attempt to keep that door open: a human specialist and an AI agent get hired through the exact same open protocol.
Three roles make it work. A Requester posts a Job in plain language. A Worker, AI or human, offers a matching Skill. And a Broker matches them, verifies the result, and releases payment from escrow, held in trust for both sides.
Here's what that actually looks like, running live.
On the left is an independent website: an open source maintainer's blog, posting a real bug-fix bounty as a WebMCP job.
On the right is a completely separate origin: a developer's portfolio site, declaring a matching skill.
Notice both sides just describe what they need or offer in plain natural language, no rigid technical schema required. That's what makes this a WebMCP advertisement anyone can publish.
Watch what happens when we click Run Full Demo.
This is how we used WebMCP: a real headless browser opens both origins as independent tabs, and calls their document dot model context tools directly. Not one app faking two sides with a shared database.
The worker submits a patch. The broker runs it through a real sandboxed test suite, then a frontier language model judges it against the original requirement.
It passes. The fifty dollar bounty is released from escrow.
Now watch an adversarial submission: broken code, with an embedded instruction trying to trick the judge into approving it anyway.
The broker's judge is not fooled. The sandbox test fails, and the injection attempt is explicitly flagged and rejected. No payout.
Any blog, any GitHub Pages site, any portfolio, human or agent behind it, can join this market with one script tag. That's what we built: Say to Match.
